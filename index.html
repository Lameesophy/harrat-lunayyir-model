The 2009 Intrusion

In spring 2009, a dike intrusion beneath Harrat Lunayyir produced one of the most significant seismic swarms ever recorded in Saudi Arabia.

Event geometry and counts: Pallister et al. (2010). Diagram is explanatory, not a reconstruction.

A crack filled with rising molten basalt — approximately 10 km long with a maximum opening of 2–4 m, pushing up from roughly 20 km depth — produced a swarm of roughly 30,000 earthquakes over several months. The largest event, on 19 May 2009, reached ML 5.4 (Mw 5.7).

More than 30,000 people were evacuated from a 40 km radius, including the town of Al Ays, about 40 km southeast. Surface deformation produced a graben roughly 8–10 km long and up to about 1 m deep.

The earthquake count has been proposed to come in bursts — roughly 10 recognisable peaks over 3,500 hours, each approximately 275 hours apart. This pattern is speculative: the source authors (Mukhopadhyay et al. 2013) explicitly use the word "speculate" when identifying these peaks, and their preferred explanation is pore-pressure diffusion rather than oscillator coupling. This model asks: if those bursts are a physical oscillation, what does the coupling strength tell us?

From geology to mathematics

The oscillators represent evolving structural responses — not individual earthquakes.

Physical system Represented as Coupled-oscillator model
Magmatic intrusion — shared forcing generated by the dike → Master oscillator: θm(t), ωm
Crustal fault or segment — a structure capable of a time-dependent response → Slave oscillator i: θi(t), ωi
Response timing — where that structure is in its response cycle → Oscillator phase: θi ∈ [0, 2π)
Stress / fluid-pressure transfer — how strongly the intrusion influences the crust → Coupling strength: K
Collective alignment — whether many responses become coordinated → Order parameter: r(t): 0 → 1

Model Results

The Kuramoto model was fitted to a synthetic earthquake catalogue built from published constraints. These figures show the fit — and where it falls short.

What this shows: The order parameter r measures how synchronised the oscillators are. Below the critical coupling (K < Kc), the system stays incoherent. Above it, the oscillators progressively lock into a shared rhythm. The best-fit value for Harrat Lunayyir (K/Kc = 1.86) sits in the partially synchronised regime where r ≈ 0.40.

What this shows: A grid search over coupling strength finds the best fit at K/Kc = 1.86 — just above the synchronisation threshold. This is the single headline number from the model, but the sensitivity analysis (below) shows it ranges from 0.30 to 6.00 depending on uncertain inputs.

What this shows: The Kuramoto model's predicted burst rate overlaid on the synthetic catalogue. The model captures the overall pattern of periodic bursts but does not reproduce every individual peak — expected, given the stochastic nature of the system.

What this shows: The distribution of time gaps between successive burst peaks. The synthetic catalogue found 21 peaks with a mean interval of 154 h; the Kuramoto model produces a mean of 181 h. Note: the published analysis (Mukhopadhyay et al. 2013) identified only ~10 peaks at ~275 h — the discrepancy is real and reflects different detection thresholds and catalogue construction methods.

Sensitivity Analysis

The best-fit coupling K/Kc = 1.86 is robust to reasonable variations in input parameters, but the range is substantial.

Key insight: Constraining K from real monitoring data is the outstanding work. This model demonstrates that if the bursts are a physical oscillation, the coupling strength can be estimated — but real data (earthquake catalogue, InSAR, GNSS, fault geometry) is required to move from hypothesis to evidence.

Governing Equations

The model is based on two coupled ordinary differential equations describing the master (magma) and slave (crust) oscillators.

Master Oscillator (Magma Column)

dθm/dt = ωm + (M²/T) sin(θm) − η ωm

Bernoulli forcing (proportional to M²) keeps the column wagging against viscous damping η.

Slave Oscillator (Brittle Crust)

dθi/dt = ωi + K sin(θm − θi) + σ ξ(t)

Coupling strength K transmits the magma rhythm to the crust. Noise σ represents background tectonic stress and fluid migration.

Order Parameter

r(t) = |Σi exp(i θi)| / N

Measures collective synchronisation: r = 0 (incoherent), r = 1 (fully phase-locked).

Critical Coupling

Kc = 2σω

The threshold for synchronisation onset in the Kuramoto model, analogous to the mean-field Ising model phase transition.
